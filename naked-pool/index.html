Your Naked pool

How your Naked Freshwater System keeps the water clean, the numbers to keep it in range, and the products you must never add.

Copper 0.2–0.5 ppm. pH 7.2–7.6.

How it works

Instead of a salt chlorinator, your pool has a Naked cell. It does three jobs as the water passes through.

Pool water to the pump Treated water back to the pool Copper rods Silver rod
Copper ions

Stop algae

Copper is the main sanitiser that stays in the water. Keeping it in range is what keeps the pool clear.

Silver ions

Control bacteria

Silver works alongside the copper to keep the water hygienic.

Oxidiser

Break down organics

A small amount of chlorine, made in the cell, burns off body oils, sunscreen and debris.

Keep the water in range

The Naked system sanitises the water, but it doesn't balance it. These are the ranges Naked recommends.

Test copper in the shade, and only once pH is in range, because high pH hides copper and gives a false low reading. Ask us to include phosphates when you bring in a sample.

Never add these

  • Stabiliser (cyanuric acid)
  • Granular chlorine
  • Copper-based algaecides, or winter algaecide packs
  • Flocculants, including aluminium-based floc
  • Bromine products
  • Soda ash (sodium carbonate)
  • Zeolite or Zelbrite filter media

What you can use

  • Lower pH: liquid hydrochloric acid, no more than 500 mL at a time
  • Raise alkalinity: sodium bicarbonate (buffer). 200 g per 10,000 L raises it by about 10 ppm
  • Raise calcium: calcium chloride, as we advise
  • Top up minerals: pool salt or mineral mix
  • Chlorine: liquid chlorine only, and only when needed

Settings and run times

Controller settings

  • ION starts at 20%, and halves automatically when the water is below 20°C.
  • OXI starts at 100%. Covered pools often need less.
  • Winter Mode halves both outputs for the cooler months.
  • Turn ION down if copper reads above 0.5 ppm. Don't keep adding more.

Pump run times

  • Timer 1 sanitises: about 1 hour per 10,000 L in summer.
  • Timer 2 circulates for 2–4 hours, starting an hour after Timer 1.
  • Variable speed pump on low? Run it for at least 8 hours a day.
  • Lots of trees or heavy use? Extend Timer 1.

Copper and stains

Too much copper, or a pH that drifts up, makes copper drop out of the water and settle on the surface as blue-green, grey or black stains. Removing them takes acid treatments and days of filtering, so prevention pays.

  • Keep copper between 0.2 and 0.5 ppm. Above 0.5, turn the ION output down.
  • Keep pH between 7.2 and 7.6, and test it before copper.
  • Never add a copper algaecide on top of the ioniser.
  • Spot a stain? Switch the ioniser off and bring us a photo and a water sample before you treat it. Our stain guide has the full process.

Cloudy or green water

Shocking a Naked pool works differently. Skip granular chlorine and floc, and work through these steps.

  1. Check pH, then copper

    Bring pH to 7.2–7.6 first, then test copper. Low copper is the most common reason a Naked pool turns green, often after heavy rain or a backwash has diluted it.

  2. Clean up

    Scoop out debris, brush the pool, empty the baskets and clean the filter.

  3. Run OXI Boost for cloudy water

    OXI Boost runs the oxidiser hard for 24 hours to clear cloudiness after storms or heavy use. Check pH again afterwards.

  4. Run ION Boost if copper is low

    OXI Boost doesn't raise copper. ION Boost does, but it only runs during Timer 1 and can take a few days to bring copper back into range.

  5. Extend the pump run time

    Run the pump longer while the pool recovers, so the cell and filter can do their work.

  6. Still green? Bring us a sample

    We'll test copper, phosphates and the balance, and if needed give you a liquid chlorine dose to knock the algae back. High phosphates feed algae and use up copper.

Looking after it

Daily
  • Look for cloudiness or any change from yesterday.
  • Keep the water above the skimmer opening, as summer evaporation can be 5 mm a day.
Weekly
  • Test pH and copper.
  • Empty the skimmer and pump baskets, and brush the pool.
Every 4–6 weeks
  • Test alkalinity and phosphates, or bring in a sample.
Every 8–10 weeks
  • Backwash or clean the filter, then check copper, as backwashing dilutes it.
As needed
  • Clean the OXI cell when white calcium builds up on the plates: switch off at the wall, then soak it for 10 minutes in 1 part hydrochloric acid to 10 parts water, with gloves and eye protection. Or bring it in.
  • Replace the ION rods about every 3 years, or when the controller shows CHECK IONISER RODS.
