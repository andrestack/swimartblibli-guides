Stains on your pool?

Most pool stains come from one of two places: metals in the water, or leaves and algae sitting on the surface. Work out which one you have, then treat it the right way.

Test it first, then treat it.

What kind of stain is it?

Colour and location give you a good first guess. The home test on the next page confirms it.

Organic

Looks like
Brown, tan or green-brown patches, sometimes leaf-shaped. Berries can leave pink or purple marks.
Found
Under leaves, gumnuts and berries, on steps and the floor.
Why
Leaves and algae left sitting in the pool release tannins that dye the surface.

Clue: chlorine lightens it.

Iron and manganese

Looks like
Rust brown, orange or reddish-brown. Manganese shows as purple to black.
Found
A brown haze across the surface, or rust spots.
Why
Bore or tank water, rusting fittings or steel in the concrete, and low pH eating away at metal parts.

Clue: vitamin C lightens it.

Copper

Looks like
Blue-green, teal, grey or black. Often blotchy on pebble finishes.
Found
On the floor and near the return jets.
Why
Copper ionisers such as Naked pools running too high, pH drifting up, or copper-based algaecides. Low pH can also eat copper parts in older heaters.

Clue: vitamin C lightens it.

Calcium scale

Looks like
White or grey crust that feels rough to touch.
Found
Along the waterline tiles, on pebble and around water features.
Why
High calcium hardness and pH, made worse by evaporation and heat.

Clue: neither test shifts it. It's a deposit, not a stain.

The two-minute stain test

You'll need a vitamin C tablet, an old sock and a spoonful of granular chlorine.

Rub vitamin C on it

Hold a vitamin C tablet on the stain for 30 seconds.

It lightens: a metal stain, most likely iron or copper.

Try chlorine

Put a spoonful of granular chlorine in a sock and hold it on the stain for 30 seconds.

It lightens: an organic stain.

Neither works

It may be calcium scale, a mix of stains, or a surface problem.

Next: bring us a photo and we'll take a look.

Skip the chlorine test on vinyl liners and coloured or patterned finishes, as it can bleach them.

Before you treat, bring us a water sample

We'll test pH, alkalinity, calcium, chlorine, iron and copper, look at your photo, and match the right product and dose to your pool. Stain removers are strong acids, so getting the dose right matters.

Treating a metal stain

  1. Confirm the stain and test the water

    Bring us a photo and a water sample. We'll confirm the stain type, check the balance, and give you the product and dose for your pool.

  2. Switch off a copper ioniser

    If you have a Naked pool or another copper ioniser, switch it off before you start and leave it off until the water is rebalanced.

  3. Let the chlorine drop

    Turn off the salt chlorinator a day or two before treating. Chlorine needs to be below 0.5 ppm for most products, as chlorine fights the stain remover.

    Short on time? We can supply a chlorine reducer.

  4. Set the pH

    Bring the pH to 7.2–7.6, or down to 7.0 for CU Later.

  5. Add the stain remover with the pump running

    Sprinkle it over the stain or evenly around the pool. On Quartzon, coloured pebble, coloured marble, exposed aggregate or printed vinyl, dissolve it in a bucket of pool water first so it never sits on the surface.

  6. Run the pump for 24 hours

    Keep swimmers out while the treatment works. Brush any stubborn spots to help it along.

  7. Rebalance slowly

    Stain removers drop pH and alkalinity. Raise them a little at a time with buffer, as raising them too fast can cloud the water and put the stain straight back.

  8. Add Aquaguard

    Once the water is balanced, add Aquaguard. It locks up the metals the stain remover has released so they can't settle back on the surface.

  9. Sand filter? Add Filter Aid & Metal Remover

    It helps the sand catch the fine metal particles so the filter takes them out of the pool.

  10. Stay on top of the filter

    The filter is now collecting the metals. Hose out a cartridge almost every day until the water is clear and the pressure stays steady. On a sand filter, backwash as soon as the flow starts to drop, which also flushes out the filter aid.

  11. Restart your system

    Turn the chlorinator back on. If you have a copper ioniser, restart it at a lower output than before and bring us a sample after a week so we can check the copper level. From then on, add Stain & Scale Defence monthly.

Handle stain removers safely

  • Wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Never mix stain removers with chlorine or other chemicals, and add each one to the pool separately.
  • Store them in their original containers, out of reach of children.
  • Keep swimmers out until the treatment has run and the water is rebalanced.

Treating an organic stain

  1. Scoop out leaves and debris, and empty the baskets.
  2. Brush the stain hard.
  3. Raise the chlorine with a shock dose at dusk.
  4. Hold granular chlorine in a sock on stubborn marks, except on vinyl or coloured finishes.
  5. Still there after a few days? Try Multi Stain Remover.

Copper and Naked pools

Copper ionisers release a small, controlled amount of copper to keep algae away. When the output is set too high, or pH and alkalinity creep up, that copper drops out of the water and settles on the surface as blue-green, grey or black stains.

  • Keep pH at the lower end, 7.2–7.4.
  • Run the ioniser at the lowest output that keeps the pool clear.
  • Don't add copper-based algaecides.
  • Bring in a sample monthly so we can check the copper level.

Stop stains coming back

  • Keep pH between 7.2 and 7.6High pH drops metals out of the water. Low pH eats metal parts and puts metals into it.
  • Use Stain & Scale Defence monthlyIt holds metals and calcium in the water so they don't settle.
  • Test bore and tank waterTop-up water from a bore or tank can carry iron and manganese. We'll test it for you.
  • Don't let leaves sitScoop leaves and berries out promptly, and use a cover if trees overhang the pool.
  • Watch your copper ioniserLower the output if copper reads high, and avoid copper algaecides.
  • Call us about rust spots that keep returningA rust spot that comes back in the same place can mean steel in the concrete is showing through.
